## 1. The short version

1. **beta increases LD entrainment in this model, strongly and monotonically, in every
   species.** *S. grossa* goes from 16.2% entrained at beta = 0 to 60.4% at its published
   beta = 0.75 and 71.8% at beta = 2.0 (Track 1). The expectation on record in the
   09 Sep handoff ("flat or declining in beta") was an amplitude result; it does not
   hold for entrainment. beta acts on period-locking, which the DD amplitude sweeps could
   never see.
2. **With the published parameters and clocks calibrated to the real DD periods, the model
   predicts the S. grossa deficit does not exist.** At published beta: *L. cornutus* 64.0%
   (observed 90.6), *A. pennsylvanica* 21.4% (observed 63.4), *S. grossa* 60.4% (observed
   48.4). The model matches *S. grossa* and under-predicts the other two. The published
   beta values for *L. cornutus* (0.09) and *A. pennsylvanica* (0.06) are too small to
   entrain clocks that free-run at 25.65 ± 1.79 h and 22.58 ± 4.23 h.
3. **Why: light in this model can only speed the clock up.** Light raises k2, so the
   range of entrainment extends only above 24 h (Fig 2). Raising beta widens it upward
   but never downward. *S. grossa* has the widest range (entrains clocks out to 34 h) but
   its individuals with tau_DD below about 22 h free-run at any beta, and *A. pennsylvanica*,
   whose period distribution is the widest and centred below 24 h, cannot reach its
   observed 63% at any beta on the grid (max 62.8% at beta = 2.0).
4. **In the data, the S. grossa–A. pennsylvanica difference is mostly detectability, not
   period-locking.** Decomposing the published criterion (`is_significant & is_entrained`
   in the Figure 1 circular-stats files): significant 71.0% vs 87.8%, in-window given
   significant 68.2% vs 72.2%. Conditional on a detectable rhythm the two species are
   nearly equal. *S. grossa* is the least active species (median activity 0.11 vs 0.32 for
   A.p in LD), and low-count animals fail the Lomb-Scargle FAP test. The model has no
   count-based detection step and cannot reproduce this part.
5. **The spider criterion scores free-running animals as entrained.** At beta = 0 (no
   entrainment possible) 54.4% of simulated *L. cornutus* pass "LS period in 22.8–25.2 h",
   because their free-running periods sit near 25 h; the exact phase-locking criterion gives
   1.0%. The gap between the two columns is the measurement artifact, quantified with no
   free parameters.
6. **Robust to the population model.** Two different ways of generating arrhythmic
   individuals (Track 1: asymmetric k; Track 2: individual output strength drawn from the
   observed spread of total activity) and a halved period spread all give the same picture
   (section 5).
7. **Waveform QC:** no non-oscillatory clocks, ≤ 5% damped, ≤ 7% sharp edges. DD bout
   widths match the spiders (FWHM 4–7 h vs 4–5 h). LD bouts are wider than the spiders'
   (7–10 h vs 2–4 h) for a structural reason: every real species shows a sharp burst of
   activity in the first hour after lights-off that the model cannot produce, because it has
   only negative masking. This is the one modification I did NOT make autonomously
   (section 7).

## 2. Data corrections made before calibrating

- **Eleven of the 45 *L. cornutus* DD animals have `Mean_Activity == 0` and
  `Total_Crossings < 50`** (`LC_spider_analysis_comprehensive_with_LD_split.csv`). All
  eleven are "Not Rhythmic". An animal with no beam crossings for eight days is an empty
  channel or a dead animal, not an arrhythmic clock, and they were removed from the
  denominator. *L. cornutus* DD rhythmic is therefore **30/34 = 88.2%**, not 66.7%. No
  other species/condition has zero-activity rows.
- Consequence for the 09 Sep reframing: the "+24.8 point gain in LD" for *L. cornutus*
  was those eleven channels. Corrected DD→LD changes are +2.4 (L.c), −32.3 (A.p),
  −20.2 (S.g).
- Authoritative source of 91/63/48 is `original figures\Figure 1 LD analysis\
  *_circular_stats_v2.csv`, rule `is_significant & is_entrained`, giving 90.6 / 63.4 / 48.4.

(`results_consolidated.csv`). Track 2 (`s1c2_population_outputvar.py`) was added after
the waveform QC: it keeps every clock nearly symmetric (sigma_asym = 0.10, bouts as narrow

## 6. Waveform QC (`qc_*.csv`, `qc_*.png` in each track folder)

Per individual at published beta, Track 1: non-oscillatory clocks 0.0%; damped in DD
2.4 / 1.0 / 5.4%; edges steeper than half the amplitude per 0.1 h 0.0 / 6.4 / 0.0%.
"Damped in LD" (18% for L.c) and "beating" (54%) are day-to-day amplitude wobble from
relative coordination of non-entrained clocks with the 24 h masking signal, not decay.

Shape against the spiders' aligned profiles (`spider_shape_reference.csv`, metrics that
are robust to count noise):

| | DD FWHM model / spider (h) | LD FWHM model / spider (h) | LD vector strength model / spider |
|---|---|---|---|
| L. cornutus | 5.4 / 5.0 | 8.4 / 3.7 | 0.55 / 0.63 |

| S. grossa | 6.7 / 4.0 | 7.6 / 2.1 | 0.23 / 0.46 |

DD is representative. LD is not, for two reasons visible in `qc_waveforms.png`: every real

activity, and the model, whose only light effect on output is suppression with a 2 h time
constant, peaks 4–8 h later; and non-entrained model individuals smear when folded at
24 h. Spider hourly max-jump and peak-count metrics are dominated by count noise and were
not used as QC criteria.

## 7. What I did and did not change, and the decision that is yours

Changed during the run: the L. cornutus DD denominator (section 2); protein rather than
locomotor peaks as the exact period (the L. cornutus gate can give a shoulder peak within a
cycle); a batch Lomb-Scargle identical to astropy for speed; the additional Track 2
population. The species parameters (beta, alpha, L_base, tau_h, kappa, y_th) were not
touched.

Not changed, because it is a change to the model rather than to the simulation: the model
cannot produce the lights-off burst, and its light input is unidirectional. Both point the
same way. A phase-dependent light action (a proper PRC with delays as well as advances)
would let short-period clocks entrain and would raise A. pennsylvanica; a positive masking
term at lights-off would sharpen the LD bouts. Either is S5-territory and should be your
call. A cheap intermediate test that needs no new mechanism is an alpha × beta run (S4).

## 8. What this means for R1-1

The reviewer's premise, that S. grossa has the highest light sensitivity yet the lowest
entrainment, is not something the model produces: given its beta, the model entrains
S. grossa well, and given their betas it entrains the other two poorly. The published
betas for L. cornutus and A. pennsylvanica are inconsistent with their own DD periods and
LD entrainment under this light mechanism; L. cornutus needs beta ≈ 0.4 to reach 90.6%.
Meanwhile in the data, about half of the S. grossa deficit relative to A. pennsylvanica is
that fewer S. grossa reach a significant periodogram at all (71% vs 88%), which is an
activity-level and record-length effect, not a clock effect. A defensible reply would (i)
report the beta sweep as showing beta helps, (ii) attribute the S. grossa deficit to
detectability plus period distribution rather than to light sensitivity, and (iii) present
the beta values as weakly identified by the original fit. The LL periods offer a weak
independent check of beta's sign: S. grossa shortens in LL (median 24.6 → 21.9 h,
n = 24/17), as the model predicts for large beta, while A. pennsylvanica lengthens
(23.8 → 23.1 median, means 22.6 → 23.9), and L. cornutus has no significant LL rhythm at
all (0/31; activity collapses to 0.05, consistent with alpha = 0.9).
